How we speak with families

Words that comfort, not words that hurry

Well-meant phrases can make a grieving person feel unheard. Every member of our team is trained to listen first and to say what is true and kind, without clichés.

What we say

  • “I'm so sorry about your mam. What was she like?”
  • “Take all the time you need. There is no rush.”
  • “There's no right or wrong way to do this.”
  • “Would it help if I explained what happens next?”
  • “Tell me about him. We'd love to make the day sound like him.”

What we avoid, and why

  • Phrases that suggest the loss is for the best, or that tell you where your loved one is now. Beliefs are yours to share, not ours to assume.
  • “I know how you feel.” We don't. Every loss is different.
  • “At least…” Nothing should be made smaller.
  • “You need to be strong.” You don't have to be anything.
  • Talking about “the body” or “the deceased”. We use their name.
