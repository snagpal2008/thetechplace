Questions families ask

Frequently asked questions

If your question isn't here, please call us. There is no such thing as a silly question.

How soon after a death does the funeral take place?

In Ireland, usually within two to four days. If the coroner is involved, or family are travelling from abroad, it may take longer. We will plan around what your family needs.

How much does a funeral cost?

Our prices are published on our prices page, and you will always get a written, itemised estimate. Third-party costs such as the grave, crematorium and church offering are extra and vary by area.

Can we have a wake at home?

Yes. We will prepare your loved one, bring them home, and provide anything you need, such as stands and candles. We can also arrange reposing in our private chapel.

What is a direct cremation?

A simple cremation without a funeral service beforehand. Families often hold a memorial or gathering later. See direct cremation.

Why might the coroner be involved?

If a death is sudden, unexpected or unexplained, the doctor must report it to the coroner. This is routine. It may mean a short delay before the funeral, and we will keep in contact with the coroner's office for you.

When and where do we register the death?

Within three months, with any Civil Registration Service office. The funeral can take place before the death is registered. We will tell you what documents to bring.

How do we place a death notice on RIP.ie?

We place notices on RIP.ie for the families we look after, and we read the wording back to you before it goes live. You can also send us the details online.

Can I plan and pay for my own funeral in advance?

Yes. See planning ahead. You can record your wishes free of charge, and paying in advance is optional.

Do you look after families of all faiths and none?

Yes. We arrange Catholic, Church of Ireland, Muslim, Hindu, humanist and non-religious funerals, among others, and we respect every tradition.
